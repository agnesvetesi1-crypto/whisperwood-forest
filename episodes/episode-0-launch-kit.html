1. Thumbnail

1280×720

Generate the character art with Higgsfield, then add the title text separately in a simple editor (Canva, Photopea) — AI image models render clean text unreliably, so title cards are always done as a second pass in real thumbnail production.

Pixar/Disney 3D animation style, bright and warm. Wide golden-hour shot in Whisperwood Forest clearing. @Coco in the foreground, mid-bound toward camera, huge joyful grin, tail flying, orange fur glowing in the light — she is the clear focal point, largest and sharpest in frame. Behind and around her, the other seven friends — @Oliver, @Benny, @Bruno, @Luna, @Hazel, @Milo, @Pip — each visible once, no duplicates, all smiling and looking toward camera, warm group energy. Soft depth of blur behind the group, sparkling sunlight through leaves, colorful flowers. Bold, high-contrast lighting so faces read clearly at small thumbnail size. Leave open sky/foliage space in the upper third for title text to be added afterward. No text, no logos.
Title ideas "Meet the Friends of Whisperwood Forest 🦊" · "Whisperwood Forest — Episode 0" · "8 New Friends to Meet!"
Why Coco up front she's the series lead and the most expressive face — leading with one big clear expression reads better at small thumbnail sizes than an even group shot.

5. Monetization checklist

YPP · COPPA

The channel can't turn on ads until it clears the YouTube Partner Program bar — but the Made-for-Kids designation below isn't optional paperwork, it's a legal requirement for content aimed at ages 3-7, and it changes what monetization looks like once you're in.

Partner Program eligibility
All of these must be true before the "Monetization" tab even appears in Studio.
Required Channel is at least 30 days old
Required 1,000 subscribers
Required 4,000 public watch hours in the trailing 12 months, OR 10M Shorts views in the trailing 90 days
Required No active Community Guidelines strikes
Required AdSense account created and linked (Studio → Earn)
Required 2-Step Verification enabled on the Google account
Required Channel located in a supported YPP country
"Made for Kids" designation
Legally required (COPPA) for content directed at children — not a style choice. Set it at Studio → Settings → Channel as the default, then double-check it on every individual upload's Visibility step. Since April 2026 YouTube auto-detects under-labeled child content and can override your setting, so it's safest to self-designate correctly from the first upload.
Legally required Set channel-level "Made for kids" default
Legally required Confirm the audience setting on every video before publishing
Effect Turns off personalized ads — only contextual ads can serve
Effect Turns off comments, live chat, notifications bell, end screens, cards
Effect Turns off Super Chat/Super Thanks and channel memberships
Expect roughly $1-3 RPM on a kids channel vs. $5-15 on general content — contextual-only ads pay less. That gap is the accepted cost of being a compliant kids' channel, not something a setting can recover.
AI content disclosure
YouTube's mandatory "altered or synthetic content" toggle targets realistic media that could pass for a real person, place, or event (deepfake-style content) — stylized 3D cartoon animation like this doesn't fall under it.
Not required Flipping the synthetic-media disclosure toggle
Recommended Keep the plain-language AI disclosure line in the description and channel "About" (already included above) — good transparency practice as detection rules keep expanding
Revenue beyond AdSense
Worth planning for, since kids-content ad RPM is capped by design.
Consider Merch (Made-for-Kids channels can't use on-platform Memberships, so this has to live off-platform — a separate store)
Consider Sponsorships with kid-safe, COPPA-compliant brands
Consider Book/toy tie-ins once the audience is established
