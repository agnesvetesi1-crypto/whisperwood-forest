WHISPERWOOD FOREST — EPISODE 2: "PIP SAYS SORRY" ("Pip Bocsánatot Kér")
Pixar/Disney 3D animation style throughout, 16:9, warm consistent color palette. Characters: Pip (fast-talking red squirrel), Milo (small brown spiky hedgehog). Lesson: apologizing means fixing what you broke, not just saying the word. No scares, no real danger, ages 3-7 audience. Ambient forest sound throughout, no background music unless noted.

SCENE 1 (0:00): Establishing shot of Whisperwood Forest in the golden late-afternoon light. Camera drifts slowly across a quiet clearing near a stream, sunlight filtering through the canopy, a light breeze rustling the leaves, birds calling softly in the distance. No characters visible yet. Narrator: "Every day in Whisperwood Forest is a little different — today starts with someone in an awfully big hurry."

SCENE 2 (0:15): Pip zips through the clearing in a blur of red fur, skids to a brief stop around a tree root, eyes darting everywhere with excitement, turns to camera speaking quickly: "Morning, morning, MORNING! So much to do, so little time — can't stop, gotta go, catch you later!"

SCENE 3 (0:30): Milo carefully places a final leaf on top of a small, intricately built leaf-and-twig house nestled between tree roots, humming softly, then leans back to admire it, speaking softly: "There... almost perfect. Just needed one more leaf for the roof."

SCENE 4 (0:45): Pip bursts through the same clearing at full speed, completely focused on the horizon ahead, and barrels straight through Milo's little leaf house without looking down — leaves and twigs scatter into the air behind him. He is already gone before the last leaf settles. Ambient scattering/crunching sound only, no dialogue.

SCENE 5 (1:00): Pip keeps bounding through the forest, glances back over his shoulder with a proud grin, completely unaware of what just happened, briefly faces camera mid-stride: "Fastest squirrel in the whole forest — that's me, no contest!"

SCENE 6 (1:15): Milo returns to find his little leaf house completely flattened, scattered leaves and broken twigs where it used to stand, ears drooping, eyes wide with sadness, kneels down beside the wreckage, speaking quietly: "My... my house. It's all broken."

SCENE 7 (1:30): Milo sits alone among the scattered leaves, picks up a small broken twig and turns it over in his paws, speaking softly to himself: "I picked every one of these leaves myself. Found just the right twigs."

SCENE 8 (1:45): Milo starts stacking a couple of leaves back into place, half-hearted, then sets them down again, discouraged, speaking quietly: "It's no use starting over all by myself. It won't be the same."

SCENE 9 (2:00): Pip finally slows to a stop on a sunny branch, catching his breath, glances down and notices bits of leaf and twig stuck in his fur, frowns, puzzled: "Huh... why do I have leaves stuck all over me?"

SCENE 10 (2:15): Pip dashes back along his own trail and skids to a stop at the sight of Milo sitting beside the ruined leaf house, his excited expression dropping into horror, speaking slowly: "Oh no... Milo, I— I did this, didn't I?"

SCENE 11 (2:30): Milo looks up at Pip, sad but calm, not angry, speaking quietly: "Saying sorry doesn't fix my house, Pip."

SCENE 12 (2:45): Pip kneels down to Milo's level, slow and steady this time instead of rushing, speaking earnestly: "Then let's fix it. Together. I'll gather the leaves — you just tell me exactly how you want them."

SCENE 13 (3:00): Pip and Milo work side by side in the golden late-afternoon light — Pip carefully carrying one leaf at a time instead of rushing, Milo directing and placing them, the little house slowly rising again, a little sturdier than before, warm satisfied mood. Narrator: "Pip learned that sorry is just the beginning — the rest is showing up, and helping put things right."

SCENE 14 (3:15): Pip and Milo stand together in front of the finished, sturdier leaf house, warm and happy. Pip, to camera: "There's always something new happening here in Whisperwood Forest — come back soon and see what we get up to next!" Milo smiles proudly at the house beside him.
